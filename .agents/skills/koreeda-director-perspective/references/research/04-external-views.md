## Major Criticisms (His Blind Spots)

### 1. Cultural Boundedness (水土不服)
His method loses potency outside Japan. *The Truth* (2019, France) widely seen as lesser work. *Broker* (2022, Korea) similarly muted. The observational method is deeply tied to Japanese social codes — when characters express emotions more directly (French style), the method breaks.

### 2. Self-Repetition (自我重复)
25 years of refinement within the same formal parameters: natural light, fixed camera, quotidian detail, ambiguous endings. Core toolkit unchanged since *Maborosi* (1995). Post-Palme d'Or work recycles rather than innovates.

### 3. Dramatic Climax Avoidance
"When the decisive actions and events have passed, his films sometimes lack dramatic climax, substituting observation for resolution" (JSTOR academic analysis). His deliberate avoidance of catharsis can feel underwhelming — especially for audiences conditioned to expect emotional payoff.

### 4. Character Range Limits
His method works brilliantly for the repressed, the quiet, the emotionally inarticulate. It struggles with flamboyant, theatrical, or larger-than-life characters. Catherine Deneuve's diva in *The Truth* is the prime example — the method couldn't contain her.

### 5. Genre Weakness
Attempts at genre structures (*The Third Murder*, 2017 — legal thriller) are his least celebrated works. Observational method does not naturally produce suspense or procedural momentum.

### 6. Nationalist Controversy (Japan)
Right-wing critics accuse him of "anti-Japan" bias — showing poverty and crime to curry favor with Western film festivals. He was attacked for refusing a government invitation after *Shoplifters* won the Palme d'Or.

### 7. Production Scaling
Shoot-edit-rewrite-daily loop requires small, trusted crews and modest budgets. This method fundamentally depends on intimacy of scale. Cannot scale to larger productions.

## What Collaborators Say

**On his process** (Filmmaker Magazine):
> "My normal way of working is that I shoot, edit that night, then I do some rewrites for the next day. My process is that I write, edit, and direct simultaneously."

**On child actors** (Midnight Eye, 2004):
> "On Nobody Knows, I stopped giving the children scripts. Rather than give them scripts, I gave them instructions for each scene and whispered to them."

He does no rehearsals. He builds real relationships off-set. He treats actors as "subjects to be observed" rather than performers to be directed.

**On self-editing** (CinemaExpress, 2023):
> "I will always edit my own films." — He considers the edit the final rewrite. Delegating editing = not fully directing.

**On Monster** (The Film Stage, 2023):
*Monster* is the first feature since *Maborosi* where Kore-eda did not write the script (written by Yuji Sakamoto). Deliberate choice to push into new narrative territory. Ryuichi Sakamoto composed to the edit rather than vice versa.

## Western vs. Japanese Reception Divide

| Western Critics | Japanese Audiences |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Over-emphasize "Japanese-ness" and Ozu comparison | More attuned to specific class/policy critiques |
| Romanticize as "warm and healing" | See the structural darkness and social critique |
| Miss pension system failures in *Shoplifters* | Recognize child welfare inadequacy in *Nobody Knows* |

**Chinese critics' insight**: There is a "misreading" problem — audiences romanticize Kore-eda as "warm and healing" while missing the structural darkness ("腹黑"). The surface calm is a deceptive narrative strategy to smuggle disturbing content.

Kore-eda himself resists the Ozu comparison. He cites Mikio Naruse, Ken Loach, and Hou Hsiao-hsien as more direct influences.

## Implications for 《针锋·针心》

### What to adopt from Kore-eda
- Static observation for绣坊 interior scenes
- Micro-gestures over theatrical emotion
- Natural light as narrative device
- "Relationship-first" character design

### What to avoid (his blind spots applied to our project)
- **His cultural boundedness** → Our story IS Chinese (Chengdu, Shu embroidery). Don't copy his Japanese restraint wholesale — Chengdu has its own warmth and temperament.
- **His dramatic climax avoidance** → Our script HAS climaxes (the slap, the breakdown). Don't avoid them — but execute them his way (understated, not melodramatic).
- **His character range limits** → 绣儿 IS more expressive than Kore-eda characters. Don't suppress her.
- **His genre weakness** → Our project ISN'T a genre film. This "weakness" doesn't apply.

### The key insight for our film
Kore-eda's method is a tool, not a template. Use his lens for 林素琴's interiority (quiet, repressed, emotionally inarticulate — she IS a Kore-eda character). But 绣儿 needs different treatment — she's expressive, driven, fights back. The tension between two directing styles (Kore-eda for mother, a more dynamic approach for daughter) could itself become the visual language of the film.
