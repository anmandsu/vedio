# Cinematography Research

## 9:16 Camera Doctrine

Vertical short drama should be designed as its own language. The Bilibili craft subtitle strongly suggests that vertical shooting weakens the conventional middle shot and pushes the grammar toward closeups, extreme closeups, upper-body closeups, expression montage, multi-angle edits, and short line delivery.

## Frame Rules

- Face scale is the default. Use closeup, extreme closeup, and upper-body closeup as the main shot sizes.
- Environmental information must be compressed into vertical layers: foreground object, face, background status symbol.
- Use low angle sparingly for 陆之琛 and power reversals; if every shot is low angle, power stops reading.
- Use insert shots as punctuation: veil, nail in palm, phone camera, high heel, black leather shoe, wedding aisle, security earpiece.
- Keep one clear focal subject per frame. 9:16 collapses crowds quickly.

## Lighting Rules

- Wedding exterior/day: bright high-end daylight, clean specular highlights, red carpet color pop, no muddy shadows.
- Car interior: slightly cooler, controlled reflection on veil and cheekbone; the heroine's eyes must read through the bridal veil.
- Power entrance: reduce background exposure slightly and give the man crisp suit edges.
- Revenge action: contrast can rise at the impact beat, then return to clean short-drama readability.

## AI Image Notes

- For image-2 prompts, specify 9:16 vertical frame, phone-drama closeup grammar, and one readable object anchor.
- Avoid cinematic wide shot language unless the shot is explicitly an establishing beat.
- Use premium Chinese vertical short drama still as a recurring style phrase, not arthouse film still.
