## Category: Animation / Visual Flow (style transfer, not photographic)

## Master Style Prompt (apply to all 6 segments)

A flowing visual poem about the 3000-year history of Shu embroidery. Style: hand-drawn ink wash meets silk thread texture, watercolor diffusion with fine needle-line details. NOT science fiction. NOT epic trailer. This is a family letter told in images. The pacing is "at a walking speed" — unhurried, meditative.

Color arc: warm earth tones (ancient bone needles) → golden red (Han-Tang golden age) → cool blue-white (digital reconstruction) → warm gold (AI completion of grandmother's unfinished piece).

### Segment 1: Shang-Zhou — Bone Needle
Abstract minimal composition. A bone needle pierces coarse primitive fabric. Texture: rough, earthy, archaeological. Colors: warm umber, clay, bone white. Grainy texture like excavated artifacts. Single needle, single thread.

### Segment 2: Han Dynasty — Silk Brocade
Geometric loom patterns emerge. Warp and weft lines interweave into structured beauty. Colors shifting to gold, vermillion, jade green. Texture: smooth silk sheen, woven precision. Patterns: double dragons, dancing phoenixes, ribbon-bearing birds (archaeologically accurate Shu brocade motifs).

### Segment 3: Tang Dynasty — Court Embroidery
Flowing silk garments, golden threads, peony and phoenix motifs. Colors: imperial gold, deep red, turquoise. Light plays across embroidered surfaces — the shimmer of silk in palace candlelight. Loose, flowing lines suggesting court robes in motion.

### Segment 4: Song Dynasty — Literati Aesthetics
Style shifts: ink wash painting dissolves into silk thread. Minimal, restrained — the 写意 (xieyi) spirit. A single plum blossom branch rendered half in ink wash, half in silk thread. Colors: ink black, paper white, subtle grey-green. Negative space is the composition.

### Segment 5: Ming-Qing — Workshop Life
Warm domestic scene. A traditional embroidery workshop, not unlike the one in the film, but from another century. Multiple embroiderers at work. Colors returning to warmth: wood brown, silk crimson, lamp gold. This is the "lived tradition" — not museum pieces, working hands.

### Segment 6: Three Generations of Hands — Transition
The most important segment. A continuous visual dissolve through three pairs of hands, each holding the needle in exactly the same gesture:
- Grandmother's hands (oldest, most weathered, steady)
- Lin-suqin's hands (56 years old, calloused, slightly trembling)
- Digital lines (cool blue wireframe) reconstructing the gesture
- Xiuer's finger pressing "publish" on a phone screen
Final frame: the grandmother's unfinished embroidery piece — half complete — then AI-generated silk threads (warm gold) gently weave into the empty spaces, completing it. No dramatic sparkle. Just quiet completion, like ink spreading in water.

## Negative Prompt

no sci-fi aesthetic, no explosive particles, no Hollywood trailer style, no 3D CGI, no neon, no cyberpunk. no fast cuts, no dramatic zoom. no epic orchestral score visualization. no text overlays. no Chinese flag or nationalist imagery. no AI-as-robot imagery — the technology is the silk thread itself.
