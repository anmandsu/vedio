# Cinematography Research

## Frame Doctrine

All generated stage images and later shot references use 16:9. The camera should preserve the proscenium/stage logic while making module recombination legible.

## Rules

- When showing stage design, use a straight-on or slight high-front 16:9 view so the modular word-building reads clearly.
- When actors move blocks, keep enough floor visible to reveal physical labor, handholds, wheels, and clean traffic lanes.
- When the system becomes threatening, shift through spacing and contrast rather than projection: isolate 白 in a wider frame, reduce fill only slightly, and let red warning accents do the pressure.
- When returning to warmth, use bright high-key light with transparent shadows; avoid dramatic high-contrast AI lighting because the user wants明快、高级、干净.
- When showing final composition, center the assembled glyph/tableau in a balanced 16:9 frame with room for the next visual beat, not a cropped poster shot.

## Lighting

- Overall: high-key, bright, airy, low contrast.
- Practical stage equivalent: soft overhead wash + side fill + warm front fill, with light shadows still visible on the floor.
- Warning mode: keep the white world intact; use cinnabar card faces or narrow side color, not blackout horror.

## Aspect Ratio

- Required image aspect ratio: 16:9.
- Prompt shorthand for future image generation: `wide 16:9 stage design render, full proscenium width visible, clean modular set`.
